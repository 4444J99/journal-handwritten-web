# Keep the Page, Animate the Revision

Practice from electronic literature, scholarly manuscript editions and current web design points to one model for a poet's handwritten journal. The scanned page stays the primary object, one encoded transcription drives several switchable views, and motion is kept for the material that holds the poem's real drama: cross-outs, insertions, competing drafts, and the movement of the hand. The editions most admired by scholars (the Shelley-Godwin Archive, the Beckett Digital Manuscript Project and the Faust Edition) all render one source many ways. They offer facsimile only, a diplomatic transcription with revisions visible, a clean reading text, and genetic tools that compare or replay drafts. Electronic literature supplies the expressive vocabulary these editions lack. Robert Kendall's layered *Faith* treats revision as palimpsest, *Abra* turns erasure into a reader's "spell," and *slippingglimpse* pairs an expressive mode with a legible one. The web platform can now deliver all of this from static files. GSAP, including SplitText, DrawSVG and MorphSVG, has been free since 2025. Same-document View Transitions reached Baseline in October 2025. Static IIIF tiles, Pagefind search and client-side TEI rendering all run on GitHub Pages. The most original opportunity is one that no source we found has done: animating a poet's *real* handwritten deletions and moving between the scanned hand and a font cut from that same hand. The two risks that recur across the critical record are motion that outshines the words and an implied writing chronology that was never recorded.

## Three traditions each give up a different reader control

The field divides into recognizable modes. Each one takes away some control the print reader had and turns that loss into meaning. **Author-timed kinetic text** takes away pace. Young-Hae Chang Heavy Industries' *Dakota* runs about six minutes with no pause, its words speeding up with the drums, and Jessica Pressman calls the result an "aesthetic of difficulty" that blocks close reading ([Pressman](https://smoulthrop.com/readings/pressman.pdf)). Hayles notes that YHCHI's pacing "rarely corresponds to a comfortable reading speed" ([Hayles, ELO](https://eliterature.org/pad/elp.html)). **In-place transforming hypertext** takes away fixity. Judd Morrissey's *The Jew's Daughter* keeps a single page whose passages swap in place when the reader hovers ([ELC1](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/morrissey__the_jews_daughter.html)). **Ambient drift** takes away legibility. In Cayley's *Translation* passages surface and sink across three languages ([ELC1](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/cayley__translation.html)). In Strickland's *slippingglimpse* video of moving water pushes the words around, but a separate scroll-text mode lets the reader speed up, reverse or stop ([ELC2](https://collection.eliterature.org/2/works/strickland_slippingglimpse.html)). **Generative and combinatory work** takes away a single sequence. Montfort and Strickland's *Sea and Spar Between* holds about 225 trillion stanzas, each one addressable by its coordinates ([I ♥ E-Poetry](https://iloveepoetry.org/en/2010/sea-and-spar-between-by-nick-montfort-and-stephanie/)). **Gesture-driven reading** makes the body part of the reading. In *Pry* readers pinch apart a character's eyelids to reach buried text ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pry_(novel))). In Wardrip-Fruin's *Screen* the reader bats back falling words of memory and cannot win ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_(VR_poem))).

Scholarly manuscript editions work the other way. They give readers more control. The **Shelley-Godwin Archive** offers four views of each page: Standard (image beside a diplomatic transcription), Image Only, a Reading Text that drops deletions and places additions, and raw TEI. A "Limit View" highlights one writer's hand and fades the others ([S-GA](https://shelleygodwinarchive.org/using-the-archive/)). The **Mark Twain Project's** digital notebooks, launched November 2025, let readers switch between manuscript line breaks and prose paragraphs and turn on a highlight layer with mouseover notes ([Mark Twain Project](https://marktwainproject.org/notebooks/introduction/)). The **Marianne Moore Digital Archive** shows all 122 notebooks as facing pages of image and transcription, with a TimelineJS chronology ([UB](https://www.buffalo.edu/digital-scholarship-studio-network/projects/faculty-projects/marianne-moore-digital-archive.html)).

The third tradition, the immersive literary website, uses atmosphere sparingly. *The David Whyte Experience* won Awwwards Site of the Month by keeping the poems legible and adding a single atmospheric layer of WebGL watercolors and soundscapes, chosen because watercolor's fluidity matches the poems ([Immersive Garden](https://immersive-g.com/projects/david-whyte-experience)). *60 Years in 60 Poems* generated each poem's visual from the length, loudness and frequency of its reading and navigated like "sifting through" a stack of archive artifacts ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/60-years-in-60-poems)). Recent prize winners show the same restraint. Everest Pipkin's *Anonymous Animal* (2023 Coover Award) is black serif text over an iFrame that plays fifteen minutes once an hour ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anonymous_Animal)). Halim Madi's *Borderline* (2024) uses plain web forms whose "No" checkboxes run away from the cursor ([halimmadi.com](https://halimmadi.com/borderline)). For a journal piece, the lesson across all three traditions is that **the reader's control is the main design decision**. The scholarly default (reader-paced, switchable views) should be the base layer. The e-lit modes work best as named, optional ways in.

## Revision is the journal's richest dynamic material

A journal of poems in several drafts, with cross-outs and insertions, already contains what genetic criticism and erasure poetics treat as content. Scholarly editions have settled on a visual grammar for it. S-GA shows deletions in gray, uses strikethrough only where the manuscript has one, sets additions in italics placed above or below the line, and shows overwrites as struck letters followed by their replacements ([S-GA](https://shelleygodwinarchive.org/using-the-archive/)). The **Beckett Digital Manuscript Project** marks a deletion inside a deletion with a double strikethrough and an illegible character with "x". It sets "open variants," where Beckett left alternatives undecided, as stacked subscript and superscript ([BDMP](https://beckett.flw.uantwerpen.be/exist/apps/bdmp/editorial)). It also goes past static display. "Compare sentences" lines up every version of a numbered sentence, CollateX builds alignment tables, a genetic map charts composition order, and **"Dynamic Text" animates a sentence's genesis every three seconds or on demand** (same source). The Faust Edition lays its diplomatic transcription over the facsimile and opens a line-by-line synopsis across all witnesses with one click ([DH abstract](https://dh-abstracts.library.virginia.edu/works/10121)). Pierazzo and André's Proust prototype reveals zones of a page in writing order or in reading order, and **shades each step yellow by how uncertain the editors are of the sequence** ([Pierazzo 2012](https://paolomonella.it/lincei/abstracts/pierazzo2012autour.html)).

Electronic literature makes this apparatus felt rather than just shown. Kendall's *Faith* unfolds in five states, each laid over the last so that it "engages in an argument with" it ([ELC1](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/kendall__faith.html)). Hayles calls the result "a palimpsest that visually performs the vacillations the lyric voice verbally articulates" ([Hayles](https://eliterature.org/pad/elp.html)). *Abra* (Borsuk, Durbin, Hatcher) gives readers finger "spells." **Erase removes words and leaves the gaps, Prune removes them and closes the space**, Mutate swaps in related words, and Graft adds the reader's own ([ISEA2016](https://www.isea-archives.org/symposia/isea2016/isea2016-art-events/isea2016-artists-statement-amaranth-borsuk-kate-durbin-ian-hatcher-abra-a-living-text/)). That erase/prune split matches the editor's choice between a diplomatic view and a reading-text view, and makes it a reader's toggle. Jhave's *ReRites* won the 2022 Coover Award for documenting "the performance of the private ritual of writing": two hours of human "carving" of machine output every morning ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ReRites)). Tom Phillips' *A Humument* app keeps rescanned hand-altered pages as the zoomable primary object and adds an "Oracle" that pairs two random pages ([Design Observer](https://designobserver.com/an-app-for-the-self-replacing-book/)).

Two cautions follow. First, a "clean" final view is a convenience, not the truth. Beckett's editors warn that their top-layer view can mislead where an unresolved deletion leaves a gap ([BDMP](https://beckett.flw.uantwerpen.be/exist/apps/bdmp/editorial)). Multiple drafts of the same poem should therefore be presented as **parallel witnesses that share line IDs**, not as rough versions leading to a winner. Any canonical text should be labeled as the poet's choice. Second, handwriting carries no timestamps. *Repentirs*, a 2026 poem whose git history is the work, drew objections that its revisions were simulated rather than lived ([LinuxFr](https://linuxfr.org/users/la-main-qui-veille/journaux/repentirs-un-poeme-dont-l-historique-git-est-l-oeuvre)). A replay of the journal's revisions should mark reconstructed order as editorial, using the Proust convention of shading uncertain steps. The poet's authority helps here: unlike Shelley's or Beckett's editors, the poet can say which change came first, or can openly decline to say. We found no e-lit work that animates a poet's actual handwritten deletions, so this appears to be open ground.

## The hand itself can move, at a known cost

Making the handwriting appear to be written needs centerline paths, which scans do not contain. The standard web technique animates `stroke-dashoffset` on **open stroked SVG paths** ([Envato Tuts+](https://webdesign.tutsplus.com/sign-on-the-dotted-line-animating-your-own-svg-signature--cms-23846t)). Vivus (MIT) and GSAP DrawSVG wrap this technique, and Vivus's `scenario` mode accepts a start time and duration for each path ([Vivus](https://github.com/maxwellito/vivus); [GSAP](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/DrawSVGPlugin/)). Ordinary bitmap tracing produces outlines, which give double lines. Single strokes need centerline tracing, available in Inkscape 1.0.1+, `autotrace -centerline`, LingDong's MIT skeleton-tracing library (ported to JS/WASM and Python), or the new and still immature `centerline-svg` ([inkscape-centerline-trace](https://github.com/fablabnbg/inkscape-centerline-trace); [skeleton-tracing](https://github.com/LingDong-/skeleton-tracing); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/centerline-svg/)). Stroking the centerline directly produces a flat vector line. The better approach is to **use each centerline, drawn at about the width of the ink stroke, as an animated SVG mask over the original scan**, so the real ink, its pressure and its bleed are revealed in writing order. This is the same design as Make Me a Hanzi, which stores an outline and a median line for every stroke, and Hanzi Writer, which animates from them ([makemeahanzi](https://github.com/skishore/makemeahanzi); [hanzi-writer](https://github.com/chanind/hanzi-writer)). The labor goes into cleaning up junctions in loops and crossings and into ordering the strokes. For about 123 pages, hand-correcting a set of showcase pages is realistic. Doing every page would call for machine-learning help.

That help exists. Google's **InkSight** (Apache-2.0 code, Small-p weights on Hugging Face) turns photos of handwritten words into digital ink. In human evaluation, **87% of its outputs were valid tracings and 67% were judged plausible as human pen paths**. It needs word-level segmentation and drops or duplicates strokes on unfamiliar material ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/a-return-to-hand-written-notes-by-learning-to-read-write/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05804v4); [GitHub](https://github.com/google-research/inksight)). The license for the weights is not stated on the repo, so check the model card before relying on it. InkSight gives a *plausible* stroke order, not the true one, which is the same disclosure problem as with revision order. Generating new lines "in the poet's hand" is possible with sjvasquez's implementation of Graves 2013, which outputs SVG ([GitHub](https://github.com/sjvasquez/handwriting-synthesis)). The codebase is old, though, and it raises the authorship questions *ReRites* deals with in the open.

The custom handwriting font allows a second family of effects. Underware's **Scribo Write** is the commercial benchmark. It stores writing speed, stroke order and pauses in a variable font, and adds contextual alternates so that "every o differs" ([Underware](https://underware.nl/fonts/scribo/features); [TypeDrawers](https://typedrawers.com/discussion/comment/62437/)). A DIY font can approach that natural variation through Calligraphr's randomized variants ([Calligraphr](https://www.calligraphr.com/en/docs/tutorial2/)) or by cycling glyph variants with `calt`, where two middle variants "kill most visible repetition" ([High-Logic](https://high-logic.com/font-editor/fontcreator/tutorials/creating-handwritten-fonts-with-contextual-alternates)). For morphing, opentype.js extracts glyph paths in the browser ([opentype.js](https://github.com/opentypejs/opentype.js)), and flubber or GSAP MorphSVG can interpolate between a traced scan and the same word set in the font ([flubber](https://github.com/veltman/flubber)). Cursive joins make it hard to match one shape to another, so a crossfade with slight displacement often reads better than a strict morph. p5.js `textToPoints` turns the font into particles that can settle into the line positions on the scan ([p5.js](https://p5js.org/reference/p5.Font/textToPoints)). fontTools `varLib` can build an "ink" axis from glyphs traced at several thresholds, as long as the masters are point-compatible ([fontTools](https://fonttools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/varLib/index.html)). Animating `font-variation-settings` forces repaints, though, so this should stay on short passages ([Val Head](https://valhead.com/2020/11/15/animating-variable-fonts-with-css/)). We found **no project that morphs between a specific scanned page and a font made from that page**, which makes this pairing the work's clearest claim to originality.

## Gimmickry, inaccessibility and obsolescence are the three failure modes

The critical record is consistent. Motion is praised when the mechanic acts out the poem's subject, and criticized when its novelty outweighs the text. *Between Page and Screen* was praised for using AR "as a poetic device rather than a gimmick" ([The Marginalian](https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/04/30/between-page-and-screen-siglio/)). *Pry*'s LARB reviewer, by contrast, found readers driven "more by curiosity and wonder at the transitions" than by the story ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pry_(novel))). The design test for each effect in the journal is whether the motion means something specific. A struck line that fades back in means "this was written, then refused." A song draft that types itself out in time with its chords means "this was meant to be sung." Ink revealed along the stroke means "a hand was here." Effects that only decorate should be cut.

Accessibility pushes in the same direction. WCAG 2.2.2 requires a pause mechanism for motion that starts automatically and lasts more than five seconds. **Scrolling an element into view or hovering over it counts as starting automatically**, while clicking a button does not ([W3C](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/pause-stop-hide)). Starting each performance from a "play this page" control avoids most of that requirement. GSAP recommends `gsap.matchMedia()` for reduced-motion branches plus an in-page toggle, because many users never set the OS preference. Its SplitText hides split characters from screen readers by default, but that breaks nested links ([GSAP a11y](https://www.gsap.com/resources/a11y/)). WebGL text should keep transparent DOM text underneath so it stays readable and selectable ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/06/05/how-to-create-responsive-and-seo-friendly-webgl-text/)). Handwriting typefaces themselves are hard on low-vision and dyslexic readers, so the transcription needs a switch to a standard text face. Scans need alt text, with the transcription serving as the long description. How a screen reader handles chords set over lyrics has not been researched and needs testing.

Durability is the quiet third risk. Flash killed *The Jew's Daughter* in 2022, while *The Dreamlife of Letters* survives only through the Ruffle emulator ([ELC1 Morrissey](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/morrissey__the_jews_daughter.html); [ELC1 Stefans](https://collection.eliterature.org/1/works/stefans__the_dreamlife_of_letters.html)). The *Taper* magazine model, in which each poem is a single self-contained HTML/CSS/JS page with no external dependencies, suits a piece meant to live on etceter4 for years ([Piksel](https://22.piksel.no/2022/11/18/taper-an-online-magazine-for-tiny-computational-poems/)). Native CSS now handles a lot. Scroll-driven animations shipped in Chrome 115 and Safari 26 ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations)), but **Firefox stable still had them behind a flag as of mid-2026** ([buildmvpfast, secondary](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/css-scroll-driven-animations-replace-js-2026)), so they need `@supports` guards and a GSAP fallback ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/animation-timeline/scroll)). Same-document View Transitions are Baseline as of Firefox 144 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)), which makes the thumbnail-to-page morph safe to rely on.

## A mode suite for the journal, all deliverable as static files

The research supports building one data source with many renderings. Encode each page once, as light TEI or equivalent JSON, with zones for poems, drawings and marginalia. Mark revisions with `<del>`, `<add place="above|below|margin">`, `<subst>`, `<metamark>` for arrows, and `<alt>` for undecided stacked words ([TEI P5 ch. 12](https://tei-c.org/release/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/PH.html); [Semlak](https://dixit.uni-koeln.de/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/Camp2-10-Martina_Semlak_-_Genetic_Editing__talk.pdf)). Give each poem an ID and each line a number so that drafts line up, following BDMP's sentence IDs. Every mode below then reads from that source. The table pairs each mode with its precedent and the tooling it would use.

| Mode | What the reader experiences | Precedent | Static-site tooling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facsimile | Deep-zoom scan, lines highlighted on hover in both directions | S-GA, BDMP, Faust | IIIF Level 0 tiles + OpenSeadragon ([IIIF training](https://training.iiif.io/australia/day-two/image-servers/level0-workbench.html)); SVG or Annotorious zones |
| Diplomatic / Reading | Toggle between revisions shown (gray, struck, raised) and a clean text labeled as the poet's choice | S-GA Reading Text; Twain line-break toggle; *Abra* erase vs. prune | CETEIcean renders TEI in the browser ([GitHub](https://github.com/TEIC/CETEIcean)); EditionCrafter for a ready-made multi-layer viewer ([DH-Tech](https://dh-tech.adho.org/blog/2024/08/30/edition-crafter-dh2024/)) |
| Palimpsest replay | Scrubbable timeline through revision states, uncertain steps shaded | *Faith*; BDMP Dynamic Text; Proust prototype | GSAP timeline over the encoded `<del>`/`<add>` order |
| Ink write-on | Real ink revealed stroke by stroke on chosen pages | Hanzi Writer; Apple "Hello" | Centerline mask over scan; InkSight or skeleton-tracing, then hand cleanup |
| Hand ↔ font | Scanned word dissolves into, or morphs to, the custom font and back | Scribo; no direct precedent found | opentype.js + flubber/MorphSVG; p5 `textToPoints` |
| Draft constellation | Every draft of a poem side by side, lines aligned, changes heat-mapped | BDMP compare; Faust synopsis; Juxta ([JDH](https://journalofdigitalhumanities.org/3-1/juxta-commons/)) | CollateX or JS diff run at build time into JSON |
| Song sheet | Chord drafts rendered as transposable sheets, optionally synced to a demo recording | — | ChordPro + ChordSheetJS ([GitHub](https://github.com/bettermusic/ChordSheetJS)); Tone.js after a click ([GitHub](https://github.com/Tonejs/Tone.js)) |
| Drift / Oracle | Random or motif-weighted page pairs; optional labeled remix of the poet's lexicon | *Humument* Oracle; *Sea and Spar Between* | Build-time JSON index; Tracery (RiTa is GPL-3.0, which carries license obligations) ([Tracery](https://github.com/galaxykate/tracery); [RiTa](https://github.com/dhowe/ritajs)) |
| Search / timeline | Faceted search by date, type (poem, song, drawing) and key | Moore TimelineJS; Twain gallery | Pagefind, with a 10,000-page site searchable in under 300 kB ([Pagefind](https://pagefind.app/)) |

Two architectural choices follow from this. First, Mirador and Universal Viewer are built for browsing collections. A single bespoke journal gets more poetic control from a custom OpenSeadragon page with SVG overlays. Annotorious's OpenSeadragon plugin was archived in February 2026, so pin a version or use plain SVG ([GitHub](https://github.com/annotorious/annotorious-openseadragon)). Second, each poem should have a plain HTML transcription page as its canonical, shareable URL. *Sea and Spar Between* shows the value of making every state addressable. The dynamic modes are layered onto that page, which also keeps it robust when moved from a hosted prototype to etceter4's static files. The IIIF static-tile workflow cited above mentions a **1 GB per-repository ceiling**, so tile sizes for 123 pages should be budgeted early. Drawings should be first-class zones with captions, not gaps in the transcription. Jen Bervin and Marta Werner's *The Gorgeous Nothings* "visual transcriptions," which place each word where it sits on the page, show how layout around a drawing can be treated as part of the poem ([Jen Bervin](https://jenbervin.com/projects/the-gorgeous-nothings-edition)).

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from which effect to use to who controls the timing and what the timing claims. Scholarly editions solved legibility and evidence but rarely move. E-lit solved feeling but often sacrificed reading or survival. A poet presenting their own journal can hold both positions at once, because the poet is both the author who decides the canonical text and the only witness to the order in which things were crossed out. That authority is the project's distinctive asset. It turns the editorial uncertainty that Beckett's and Proust's editors could only shade into something the poet can state, withhold or leave open on purpose.

The unoccupied ground is clear. No source we found animates a poet's real handwritten deletions, and none moves between a scanned hand and a font cut from that same hand. A focused prototype should therefore start with the shared data model plus three modes: Facsimile with linked Diplomatic/Reading views, Palimpsest replay on a handful of heavily revised poems, and Hand ↔ font on one page. That lets the most novel mode be tested on real pages before stroke recovery is scaled to all 123.

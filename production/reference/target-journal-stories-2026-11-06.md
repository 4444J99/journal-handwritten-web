# Target-journal treatment of Instagram Stories — 2026-11-06

## Question

Does an Instagram Story (24-hour, ephemeral) count as a prior publication for
a target journal, and does expiry preserve a "Reserve" poem? Issue #68 needs
this before the 9:16 assembly is used anywhere public.

## Evidence gathered

### Rattle (fetched 2026-11-06, rattle.com/submissions and submittable)

Rattle's policy (quoted):

> "Rattle does not accept work that has been previously curated, in print or
> online — poems may be self-published on social media, blogs, or message
> boards, but cannot have been published in books, magazines, or similar
> collections open to the public."

and:

> "We want to be the first publisher to highlight the poems, but never want to
> discourage anyone from sharing their poems themselves."

Interpretation for this project:

- Rattle distinguishes **curated** publication (books, magazines, collections
  open to the public) from **self-published** sharing (social media, blogs,
  message boards). Self-published work is allowed.
- An Instagram Story is self-published sharing, not a curated collection, so it
  likely falls on the allowed side under Rattle's "uncurated" standard.
- However, Rattle's distinction is **curatorial**, not **temporal**. The 24-hour
  expiry of a Story does not convert a public appearance into a non-appearance;
  the policy does not say "removed within 24 hours is fine." A Story is also
  capturable (screenshots, replays, downloads) during its availability.
- Rattle explicitly allows social-media self-publishing, which is why it is the
  journal named in `docs/plan.md` as a venue that accepts self-posted work.

### Narrative Magazine (fetched 2026-11-06, narrativemagazine.com/submission-guidelines)

- Narrative requires "previously unpublished" work for Story of the Week and
  treats "previously published" individual poems as allowable only in a
  chapbook with an acknowledgments page.
- The fetched guidelines do not define whether a social-media Story counts as
  publication, and do not address ephemeral posts. No Story-specific rule was
  found.

### Other journals

No second journal with an explicit Story rule was found in this pass. General
poetry-journal practice (as recorded in `docs/plan.md`) is that strict journals
count **any online appearance**, including drafts, fragments and scans, and
expiry does not reliably preserve Reserve status.

## Conclusions (provisional)

1. **Do not assume expiry preserves Reserve.** A Story is a public
   distribution while it is available, and it can be captured. Treat it as an
   appearance unless the target journal's written policy says otherwise.
2. **Rattle is the permissive case**, not the rule: its "uncurated" standard
   accepts social-media self-publishing, so a Story or Reel posted to the
   journal account is unlikely to disqualify a poem from Rattle. That does not
   extend to journals with a "no prior online appearance" rule.
3. **The Reserve (15–30 poems, never online in any form) must never be posted,
   including as a Story**, until the author confirms the target journal's
   written policy. The 9:16 assembly in this kit is therefore a private
   production sample, not a published Story.
4. The finished 9:16 sample still requires the author's original Live
   Photo/video clips; the provisional layers are stills.

## What remains unresolved

- The author's final target-journal list and each journal's written rule on
  ephemeral posts (Stories, Reels, close-friends lists).
- Whether "close friends" / restricted audiences count as publication for the
  chosen journals.
- Whether a Story that is deleted before submission still counts as an
  appearance under each journal's policy.
- Whether the 9:16 assembly, if ever posted, should use a Reserve-excluded
  piece or a Series piece only.
